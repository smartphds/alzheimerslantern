## What makes an activity work

- It uses skills they still have, often skills from their past job or hobbies.
- It feels like adult activity, not a children's game.
- It's broken into simple steps.
- The goal is enjoyment, not getting it "right."

## Early stage ideas

- Walking, gardening, swimming, or exercise classes
- Cooking a favorite recipe together
- Volunteering, faith activities, or clubs they already belong to
- Puzzles, card games, and reading
- Making a life story book with photos

## Middle stage ideas

- Folding towels, sorting socks, setting the table, or watering plants
- Looking at photo albums and talking about the past
- Listening to music from their teens and twenties, and singing along
- Simple crafts, coloring, or sorting buttons or coins
- Short walks or car rides

## Late stage ideas

- Listening to favorite music
- Gentle hand massage with lotion
- Holding a soft blanket or a familiar object
- Sitting outside in the sun
- Reading aloud or a short prayer

## If they lose interest

Try a different time of day or a shorter activity. Don't push. Some days just sitting together is enough. If they seem withdrawn or sad for weeks, ask the doctor about depression.
