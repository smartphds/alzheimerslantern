## First, breathe

Hearing "Alzheimer's" is a shock, for the person diagnosed and for the family. Feeling numb, scared, angry, or even relieved to finally have an answer are all normal. Nothing on this list has to happen today. Print it, and work through it a little at a time.

## Week 1: Understand and take in the news

- **Write down your questions** as they come up. There will be many. See [Questions to ask the doctor](#questions-for-doctor).
- **Ask for a copy of the diagnosis** and any test results for your records.
- **Learn the basics** from trusted sources. Start with [What is Alzheimer's disease?](#what-is-alzheimers) and [The stages, explained simply](#stages).
- **Call the Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline at 1-800-272-3900.** It's free. A specialist can answer questions and point you to local help. You can call just to talk.
- **Let yourself feel it.** Take time together before making big decisions.

## Week 2: Talk about care and treatment

- **Make a follow-up appointment** to talk about treatment options. Ask whether the newer medicines that slow the disease might be right, and what the risks are. See [New Alzheimer's treatments in plain English](#new-treatments).
- **Review all medicines**, including over-the-counter ones and supplements. Some can make confusion worse.
- **Ask about a care plan.** Medicare covers a special visit for people with memory problems to make a care plan. Ask the doctor about a "cognitive assessment and care plan" visit.
- **Ask for referrals** to a specialist, such as a neurologist, geriatrician, or memory clinic, if you haven't seen one.

## Week 3: Legal and money planning

This matters most early, while the person can still understand and sign documents.

- **Find important papers:** wills, insurance policies, bank and retirement accounts, deeds, and passwords. Keep them in one safe place.
- **Set up a power of attorney** for money and for health care, and an advance directive (a living will). See [Power of attorney and advance directives](#power-of-attorney).
- **Consider meeting an elder law attorney**, who can explain options for paying for care later. The Eldercare Locator (**1-800-677-1116**) can help you find free or low-cost legal help.
- **Watch for scams.** Set up alerts on bank accounts and consider adding a trusted person to view the accounts.

## Week 4: Build your support team

- **Decide who to tell and how.** See [Telling family and friends](#telling-family).
- **Share the load.** Make a list of tasks (rides, meals, bills, visits) and let people choose what they can do.
- **Find a support group** for the person with the diagnosis, for caregivers, or both. See [Support groups near you and online](#support-groups).
- **Talk about safety:** driving, cooking, medicines, and what to do if the person gets lost. See [When to stop driving](#driving) and [Wandering: prevention and what to do](#wandering).
- **Plan something enjoyable.** A diagnosis doesn't end a life. Many people live well for years after an early diagnosis.

## For the person with the diagnosis

If you're the one who has been diagnosed: you still get to make choices about your life and your care. This is the best time to say what matters to you, so your family can honor it later. Many people find it helps to keep a routine, stay active, and talk with others who are living with Alzheimer's too.
