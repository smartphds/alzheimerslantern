## Why it happens

Alzheimer's damages short-term memory first. Each time they ask, it feels to them like the first time. Repeating can also be a sign of worry, boredom, or not knowing what will happen next.

## Answer the feeling

Ask yourself what they might be feeling. "When are we going?" may really mean "I'm anxious." Try:

- "We have plenty of time. I'll tell you when it's time to go."
- "You're safe here with me."

A calm, warm answer helps more than the facts.

## Practical tips

- **Answer briefly and kindly**, as if it's the first time.
- **Don't say "I already told you."** It upsets them and doesn't help them remember.
- **Write it down.** A note or whiteboard that says "Lunch with Sarah at 12" lets them check for themselves.
- **Don't announce plans too early.** Tell them about an appointment shortly before you leave, not the day before.
- **Redirect.** Offer a snack, a walk, a task, or music.
- **Look for a pattern.** Does it happen at the same time each day? Being bored or tired can make it worse.

## Take care of yourself

It's normal to feel frustrated. Step away for a moment when you need to. Talking with other caregivers in a [support group](#support-groups) can help you laugh about it instead of crying.
