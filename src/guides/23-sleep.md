## Why sleep changes

Alzheimer's affects the parts of the brain that control the body clock. People may sleep more in the day, wake often at night, or get confused about whether it's day or night. Pain, needing the bathroom, medicines, and too little daytime activity can make it worse.

## Daytime habits that help at night

- **Get morning light.** Open curtains or sit outside early in the day.
- **Stay active.** A walk, chores, or an activity they enjoy. Avoid long naps late in the day.
- **Limit caffeine** (coffee, tea, cola, chocolate) to the morning.
- **Keep a routine.** Wake, meals, and bedtime at the same times every day.

## At bedtime

- Keep the evening quiet. Soft music, a warm drink without caffeine, or a familiar TV show.
- Make sure they use the bathroom before bed.
- Keep the bedroom comfortable, with a night light so they can find their way.
- Use the bed only for sleep, so it signals rest.

## When they wake at night

- Stay calm and speak softly. Don't argue about the time.
- Check for simple needs: bathroom, thirst, hunger, pain, too hot or cold.
- Gently guide them back to bed. If they're upset, a short walk or a quiet activity in dim light can help.
- Keep doors and stairs safe in case they get up alone. See [home safety, room by room](#home-safety).

## Ask the doctor before using sleep medicine

Some sleep aids, including over-the-counter ones, can make confusion worse or raise the risk of falls. Ask the doctor to check for pain, depression, sleep apnea, or medicines that may be keeping them awake.

## Look after your own sleep

If nights are broken, find help so you can rest. A family member, a paid aide for a few nights, or [respite care](#respite-care) can make a real difference.
