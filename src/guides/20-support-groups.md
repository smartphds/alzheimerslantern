## Why support groups help

In a support group, nobody needs things explained. Members share practical tips, hard feelings, and sometimes a much-needed laugh. Many caregivers say it's the place they feel most understood.

## Where to find a group

- **Alzheimer's Association.** Free in-person and virtual groups for caregivers, and early-stage groups for people living with the diagnosis. Call **1-800-272-3900** or use the online support group finder.
- **ALZConnected.** A free online community run by the Alzheimer's Association, open any time.
- **Family Caregiver Alliance.** Online support groups for family caregivers.
- **Local hospitals, memory clinics, senior centers, and places of worship** often host groups.
- **Memory cafés.** Relaxed social gatherings for people with memory loss and their care partners, often at libraries or community centers.

## Choosing a group

- **Who's it for?** Spouses, adult children, early-stage, young-onset, or a specific type of dementia.
- **In person or online?** Online groups are great if it's hard to leave home.
- **When does it meet?** Look for a time you can manage regularly.
- **Is it led by a trained leader?** That usually helps groups run well.

It's okay to try a few groups before one feels right.

## If you can't attend a group

- Call the 24/7 Helpline whenever you need to talk.
- Join an online forum and read at your own pace.
- Ask a counselor or social worker for one-on-one support.
