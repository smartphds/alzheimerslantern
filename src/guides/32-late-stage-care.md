## What the late stage often looks like

- Needing help with all daily care
- Speaking only a few words, or none
- Trouble walking, then sitting up, then holding up their head
- Trouble swallowing
- Sleeping much of the day
- Getting infections more easily, especially pneumonia

## Comfort care

- **Skin:** Change their position at least every 2 hours if they're in bed or a chair, to prevent pressure sores. Keep skin clean and dry. Use pillows to cushion bony spots.
- **Mouth:** Clean teeth or gums daily. Keep lips moist.
- **Eating:** Offer soft or pureed foods and thickened drinks if the doctor advises it. Make sure they're sitting upright while eating and for 30 minutes after.
- **Pain:** They may not be able to tell you they hurt. Watch for frowning, moaning, restlessness, or pulling away when touched, and tell the doctor.

## Connecting without words

People in the late stage can still feel comfort and love. Hold their hand. Brush their hair. Play music they loved. Read to them, pray with them, or just sit together. Talk to them as you always have.

## Decisions you may face

The doctor may ask about feeding tubes, hospital trips, and antibiotics for infections. Research shows feeding tubes generally don't help people with advanced dementia live longer or more comfortably. Careful hand feeding is often recommended instead. These are hard choices. Lean on their advance directive and what they would have wanted, and ask about [hospice](#hospice).
