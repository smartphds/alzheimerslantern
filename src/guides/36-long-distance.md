## Ways to help from a distance

- **Take on paperwork.** Bills, insurance, Medicare and Medicaid forms, and calls to services can all be done remotely.
- **Find local help.** Call the Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116 to find the Area Agency on Aging near your loved one.
- **Support the main caregiver.** Call regularly, listen, pay for respite care, or arrange meal delivery.
- **Build a local team.** Get phone numbers for neighbors, friends, and faith community members who can check in.
- **Get permission.** Ask your loved one to sign forms that let doctors talk with you, and keep copies of key documents.

## Making visits count

- Plan ahead. Book doctor visits during your trip so you can go along.
- Look around the home for safety problems, spoiled food, unopened mail, or unpaid bills.
- Notice changes in weight, hygiene, and mood.
- Give the main caregiver real time off while you're there.
- Leave time just to enjoy being together.

## Consider a care manager

A geriatric care manager, sometimes called an aging life care professional, is a nurse or social worker who can assess needs, arrange services, and check in for you. They charge by the hour, and the cost is usually not covered by Medicare.
