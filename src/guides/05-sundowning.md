## What sundowning looks like

As the day goes on, the person may:

- Become more confused or anxious
- Pace, wander, or try to leave ("I need to go home")
- Get angry, suspicious, or tearful
- Have trouble settling down or falling asleep
- See or hear things that aren't there

It's common in the middle and late stages. It's not your fault, and it's not the person being difficult on purpose.

## What may cause it

No one knows the exact cause, but these often play a part:

- **Tiredness** at the end of the day
- **Changes in the body clock** caused by Alzheimer's
- **Fading light** and more shadows, which can be confusing or scary
- **A busy household** in the evening, with dinner, TV, and people coming home
- **Unmet needs** such as hunger, thirst, pain, needing the bathroom, or boredom
- **Infections**, especially urinary tract infections, which can suddenly make confusion much worse

## What helps

**During the day:**

- Keep a steady routine for waking, meals, and bedtime.
- Get outside or near a bright window in the morning. Daylight helps set the body clock.
- Plan active things, like walks and chores, earlier in the day.
- Limit long naps, especially late in the day.
- Cut down on caffeine and sugar after lunch, and avoid alcohol.

**In the late afternoon and evening:**

- **Turn on lights before it gets dark** to reduce shadows. Close curtains at dusk.
- **Keep things calm:** soft music, less TV, fewer visitors.
- Offer a simple, soothing activity, like folding towels, looking at photos, or a hand massage.
- Serve a lighter dinner, earlier.
- Check for pain, hunger, thirst, and the need for the bathroom.

**When it happens:**

- Stay calm and speak softly. Your mood is contagious.
- Don't argue or try to reason. Reassure instead: "You're safe. I'm right here."
- Gently redirect to a favorite snack, song, or task.
- Give space, if it's safe, and try again a few minutes later.
- Keep a simple diary of when it happens and what came before. Patterns can show you what to change.

## When to call the doctor

- If the confusion is **sudden or much worse** than usual. This may be an infection or a medicine problem, which needs checking soon.
- If sleep problems are wearing everyone out. Ask about safe options. Many sleep medicines can make confusion and falls worse in older adults, so ask before trying anything, including over-the-counter sleep aids.
