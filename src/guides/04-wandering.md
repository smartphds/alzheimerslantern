## If someone is missing right now

- **Search the immediate area quickly**, for no more than 15 minutes. Check nearby places they like and the direction of their dominant hand, which people often follow.
- **Then call 911.** Say the person has dementia and is a "vulnerable adult." You do not need to wait 24 hours.
- **Have ready:** a recent photo, what they're wearing, their medical needs, and places they might try to go, such as a former home or workplace.
- **Have someone stay home** in case they come back.
- Most people who wander are found close to where they went missing, often within a mile and a half.

## Why people wander

According to the Alzheimer's Association, **6 in 10 people with dementia** will wander at least once. Common reasons:

- Feeling lost or confused, even at home
- Trying to "go home" or "go to work," following an old routine
- Looking for a person or place from the past
- Needs like hunger, thirst, or using the bathroom
- Restlessness, boredom, or too much noise
- Evening confusion ([sundowning](#sundowning))

## Warning signs to watch for

- Coming back later than usual from a walk or drive
- Talking about going to a past job or home
- Pacing or restless movement
- Trouble finding familiar places, such as the bathroom
- Seeming lost in a new or crowded place

## Steps to take today

- **ID they always wear.** A medical ID bracelet with "memory loss" and your phone number. Programs like MedicAlert offer 24/7 help if someone is found.
- **Tell neighbors** and local shops. Give them your number and ask them to call if they see your loved one out alone.
- **Keep a recent photo** and a list of places they might go.
- **Use GPS if it fits.** Phone location sharing, GPS watches, or shoe trackers can help find someone quickly. Some areas also have programs such as Project Lifesaver through local police.
- **Make doors less inviting.** Simple locks placed high or low, door chimes, or alarms. A dark mat or a sign that says "STOP" in front of a door can also help. Never lock a person with dementia in alone. It's a fire risk.
- **Hide car keys** if the person should no longer drive.

## Lower the urge to wander

- Keep a daily routine with regular exercise. Being tired in a good way helps.
- Take care of basic needs: food, drink, bathroom, and comfort.
- Avoid busy, noisy places that can cause confusion.
- If they want to "go home," reassure them rather than correcting them: "You're safe here. Let's have some tea first."
- Plan calming activities for the times of day they're most restless.
