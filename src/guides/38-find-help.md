## In an emergency

- **Call 911** for a medical emergency or if someone is missing. Say the person has dementia.
- **Call or text 988** if you or someone else is in emotional crisis or thinking about suicide.

## Phone lines for help and advice

- **Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline: 1-800-272-3900.** Free advice from trained staff, any time of day or night, in over 200 languages.
- **NIA Alzheimer's and related Dementias Education and Referral (ADEAR) Center: 1-800-438-4380.** Free information from the National Institute on Aging.
- **Eldercare Locator: 1-800-677-1116.** Connects you with your local Area Agency on Aging for meals, in-home help, adult day programs, and caregiver support.

## Local services

- **Area Agency on Aging.** Find yours through the [Eldercare Locator](https://eldercare.acl.gov). They can tell you about local programs and caregiver support.
- **Respite care.** The [ARCH National Respite Locator](https://archrespite.org) lists respite programs by state.
- **Health insurance counseling.** Free, unbiased Medicare help from your [State Health Insurance Assistance Program](https://www.shiphelp.org).
- **Veterans.** Veterans and surviving spouses may get help paying for care. See [VA Aid and Attendance](https://www.va.gov/pension/aid-attendance-housebound/).

## Support groups and community

- [Alzheimer's Association support groups](https://www.alz.org/help-support/community/support-groups), in person and online
- [ALZConnected](https://www.alzconnected.org), a free online community
- [Family Caregiver Alliance support groups](https://www.caregiver.org/connecting-caregivers/support-groups/)

## Research studies

Clinical trials test new treatments and need volunteers, both people with Alzheimer's and healthy volunteers. Search [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov) or ask the Alzheimer's Association Helpline about matching services.
