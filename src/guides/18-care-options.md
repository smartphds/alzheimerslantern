## Start with the person's needs

Ask yourself:

- How much help does the person need with daily tasks like bathing, dressing, eating, and the toilet?
- Is it safe for them to be alone? For how long?
- Do they wander, or have behaviors that are hard to manage?
- How are you, the caregiver, holding up?
- What can the family afford, and what help is available?

## The main options

**In-home care**

- Aides come to the home for a few hours a day up to around the clock.
- "Companion" or "homemaker" help covers company, meals, and housework. "Personal care" aides help with bathing and dressing.
- Good for: people who want to stay home, and families who can fill gaps.
- Usually paid privately. Medicaid waivers or long-term care insurance may help.

**Adult day programs**

- Supervised activities, meals, and social time during the day, at a center.
- Good for: giving caregivers time to work or rest, and keeping the person active.
- Often lower cost than in-home care for the same hours.

**Assisted living**

- An apartment or room with meals, help with daily tasks, and staff on site.
- Good for: people who need some help but not constant nursing care.
- Check whether the community accepts residents with dementia and how it handles wandering.

**Memory care**

- A secure unit or community designed for people with dementia, with specially trained staff and structured activities.
- Good for: people in the middle and later stages who wander or need close supervision.
- Usually costs more than standard assisted living.

**Nursing home (skilled nursing)**

- 24-hour nursing care and supervision.
- Good for: people with complex medical needs or who need help with almost everything.
- Medicaid can pay for nursing home care for people who qualify. See [Paying for care](#paying-for-care).

## What to look for when you visit

- Is staff kind and patient? Do they speak to residents by name?
- How many staff are on duty, day and night?
- What training in dementia care do staff have?
- Are residents engaged in activities, or sitting alone?
- Is it clean, calm, and safe? Are exits secure?
- How do they handle behaviors, medicines, and medical emergencies?
- Visit more than once, including at mealtime and in the evening.

## Getting help choosing

- Your local **Area Agency on Aging** (Eldercare Locator: **1-800-677-1116**)
- A **geriatric care manager** (also called an aging life care professional), for a fee
- State inspection reports for assisted living and nursing homes
- **Medicare's Care Compare** tool for nursing homes

Moving a loved one is a hard, loving decision, not a failure.
