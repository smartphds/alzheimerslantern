## Normal forgetting vs. a warning sign

Forgetting where you put your glasses is normal. Forgetting what glasses are for is not. The difference is whether memory and thinking problems **keep getting worse** and **get in the way of everyday life**.

Below are the 10 warning signs the Alzheimer's Association lists. For each one, there's an example of what's typical with age, so you can compare. You can print this page and tick off what you've noticed before a doctor's visit.

## 1. Memory loss that disrupts daily life

Forgetting things you just learned, important dates, or events. Asking the same question again and again. Relying more and more on notes or family members for things the person used to handle.

**Typical with age:** sometimes forgetting a name or appointment, but remembering it later.

## 2. Trouble planning or solving problems

Difficulty following a familiar recipe, keeping track of monthly bills, or working with numbers. Things take much longer than they used to.

**Typical with age:** making an occasional mistake when balancing a checkbook.

## 3. Difficulty with familiar tasks

Getting lost driving to a familiar place, forgetting the rules of a favorite game, or struggling to use the microwave.

**Typical with age:** occasionally needing help with a new TV remote or phone setting.

## 4. Confusion about time or place

Losing track of dates, seasons, or how much time has passed. Forgetting where they are or how they got there.

**Typical with age:** getting confused about the day of the week, then figuring it out.

## 5. Trouble with vision and space

Problems judging distance, telling colors apart, or reading. This can lead to falls or trouble driving. (Always rule out eye problems, such as cataracts.)

**Typical with age:** vision changes from cataracts.

## 6. New problems with words

Stopping in the middle of a conversation with no idea how to go on. Struggling to find the right word or calling things by the wrong name, such as calling a watch a "hand clock."

**Typical with age:** sometimes having trouble finding the right word.

## 7. Misplacing things and not being able to retrace steps

Putting things in unusual places, such as keys in the fridge. Being unable to go back over their steps to find them. Sometimes accusing others of stealing.

**Typical with age:** misplacing things from time to time and retracing steps to find them.

## 8. Poor judgment

Giving large amounts of money to telemarketers or scams. Paying less attention to grooming or keeping clean.

**Typical with age:** making a bad decision once in a while.

## 9. Pulling away from work and social life

Losing interest in hobbies, social plans, or sports. Avoiding being social because of the changes they've noticed in themselves.

**Typical with age:** sometimes feeling tired of work or social obligations.

## 10. Changes in mood and personality

Becoming confused, suspicious, depressed, fearful, or anxious. Getting upset easily at home, at work, or anywhere outside their comfort zone.

**Typical with age:** having set ways of doing things and getting irritable when a routine is disrupted.

## What to do if you notice these signs

- **Don't wait.** Many things can cause memory problems, including medicine side effects, thyroid problems, low vitamin B12, depression, and infections. Some of these can be treated.
- **Write it down.** List which signs you've noticed, when they started, and real examples. Bring the list to the appointment.
- **Make an appointment** with a primary care doctor, and ask for a memory or cognitive evaluation. Medicare covers a yearly wellness visit that includes a check of memory and thinking.
- **Go together.** A family member can share what they've seen and help remember what the doctor says.

If it is Alzheimer's, finding out early gives more time to plan, to try treatments that work best in the early stage, and to make decisions together.
