## Hold a family meeting

- Include everyone who should be involved. Use a video call for those far away.
- If possible, include the person with Alzheimer's, especially in the early stage.
- Start with facts: what the doctor said and what care is needed now.
- Make a list of jobs, then let each person choose what they can do.
- Write down the plan and share it with everyone.
- Set a date to check in again, since needs will change.

## Jobs anyone can take on

- Driving to appointments
- Managing bills, insurance, and paperwork
- Sitting with your loved one so the main caregiver gets a break
- Paying for respite care, meal delivery, or cleaning
- Researching services and care options
- Weekly phone calls with the person and the caregiver

## Handling disagreements

- Focus on what your loved one needs and would want.
- Ask for specific help: "Can you take Mom to her Tuesday appointments?" works better than "I need more help."
- Invite family to spend a full day caregiving, so they understand.
- A social worker, counselor, or faith leader can help run a hard meeting.
