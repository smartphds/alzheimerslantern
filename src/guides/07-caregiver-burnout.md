## You are not alone

Nearly **12 million** Americans care for a family member or friend with Alzheimer's or another dementia, without pay. Many say the stress is high, and caregivers often put their own health last. Taking care of yourself isn't selfish. It's how you keep going.

## Signs of burnout

- Feeling tired all the time, even after sleeping
- Getting sick more often
- Feeling hopeless, numb, or like nothing you do is enough
- Snapping at others, or feeling angry at the person you care for
- Losing interest in things you used to enjoy
- Changes in sleep or appetite
- Pulling away from friends
- Using alcohol, food, or medicines to cope
- Thinking "I can't do this anymore"

If you're thinking about harming yourself, or someone else, **call or text 988** right now. It's free and open 24/7.

## Small first steps

You don't have to fix everything. Pick one or two.

- **Say yes to help.** When someone says "let me know if I can help," give them a specific job: "Could you sit with Dad on Tuesday afternoon?"
- **Make a list** of tasks others could do: groceries, rides, a meal, mowing the lawn, an hour of company.
- **Take short breaks every day.** Ten minutes outside, a cup of tea, a call with a friend.
- **Keep your own doctor visits.** Tell your doctor you're a caregiver.
- **Move a little.** A short walk helps mood and sleep.
- **Talk to people who understand.** See [Support groups near you and online](#support-groups).
- **Lower the bar.** A clean-enough house and a simple dinner are fine.

## Get regular time off

- **Adult day programs** give your loved one a safe, social day, and give you a break.
- **In-home help** can come for a few hours a week.
- **Short stays** in a care home, called respite care, can give you a few days to rest.

See [Finding respite care](#respite-care) for how to find and pay for these.

## Feelings you might not expect

Guilt, resentment, grief, and even moments of wishing it were over are all common. Having these feelings doesn't make you a bad person. Talking with a counselor or support group can help you carry them.

## Where to call

- **Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline: 1-800-272-3900.** Free, confidential support, any time.
- **Eldercare Locator: 1-800-677-1116.** To find local services such as respite care and meals.
- **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline:** call or text **988**.
