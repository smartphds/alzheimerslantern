Home safety, room by room

Small changes at home can prevent falls, burns, and other accidents. Print this checklist and walk through the house room by room.

By the Alzheimer's Lantern team · Last reviewed November 19, 2026 · 6-minute read

We are not medical professionals. This guide is general information, not medical advice. Please talk to a doctor about your own situation. In an emergency, call 911. Read our full disclaimer.

Throughout the house

  • Remove loose rugs and clutter from walkways.
  • Add night-lights in halls, bedrooms, and bathrooms.
  • Make sure there are working smoke and carbon monoxide detectors.
  • Lock away medicines, cleaning products, guns, and power tools.
  • Keep a list of emergency numbers by the phone.
  • Lower the water heater to 120°F (49°C) or less to prevent burns.

Kitchen

  • Install stove knob covers or an automatic shut-off device.
  • Unplug small appliances such as toasters and kettles when not in use.
  • Remove sharp knives from easy reach.
  • Check the fridge often for spoiled food.
  • Remove artificial fruit or food-shaped magnets that might be mistaken for food.

Bathroom

  • Add grab bars by the toilet and in the tub or shower.
  • Use a non-slip mat inside and outside the tub.
  • Use a shower chair and a handheld shower head.
  • Consider a raised toilet seat.
  • Remove locks from the bathroom door, so the person can't get locked in.
  • Keep razors and medicines out of reach.

Bedroom

  • Keep a clear path from the bed to the bathroom.
  • Put a lamp or light switch within reach of the bed.
  • Consider a lower bed or a soft mat beside it if there's a risk of falling out.

Stairs

  • Make sure there are sturdy handrails on both sides.
  • Add bright tape to the edge of each step.
  • Use safety gates if needed.

Doors and outside

  • Add door chimes or alarms if wandering is a worry. See Wandering: prevention and what to do.
  • Hide a spare key outside in case the person locks you out.
  • Lock up the garage, car keys, and lawn equipment.
  • Keep outdoor paths well lit and clear.

Revisit often

Needs change as Alzheimer's progresses. Walk through the house again every few months. An occupational therapist can visit and suggest changes; ask the doctor for a referral.

Sources

  • National Institute on Aging: Alzheimer's Caregiving: Home Safety Tips
  • Alzheimer's Association: Home Safety
