Find help near you

You don't have to figure this out alone. These free services can connect you with help in your area, often with one phone call. Save this page or print it.

By the Alzheimer's Lantern team · Last reviewed November 19, 2026 · 3-minute read

We are not medical professionals. This guide is general information, not medical advice. Please talk to a doctor about your own situation. In an emergency, call 911. Read our full disclaimer.

In an emergency

  • Call 911 for a medical emergency or if someone is missing. Say the person has dementia.
  • Call or text 988 if you or someone else is in emotional crisis or thinking about suicide.

Phone lines for help and advice

  • Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline: 1-800-272-3900. Free advice from trained staff, any time of day or night, in over 200 languages.
  • NIA Alzheimer's and related Dementias Education and Referral (ADEAR) Center: 1-800-438-4380. Free information from the National Institute on Aging.
  • Eldercare Locator: 1-800-677-1116. Connects you with your local Area Agency on Aging for meals, in-home help, adult day programs, and caregiver support.

Local services

  • Area Agency on Aging. Find yours through the Eldercare Locator. They can tell you about local programs and caregiver support.
  • Respite care. The ARCH National Respite Locator lists respite programs by state.
  • Health insurance counseling. Free, unbiased Medicare help from your State Health Insurance Assistance Program.
  • Veterans. Veterans and surviving spouses may get help paying for care. See VA Aid and Attendance.

Support groups and community

  • Alzheimer's Association support groups, in person and online
  • ALZConnected, a free online community
  • Family Caregiver Alliance support groups

Research studies

Clinical trials test new treatments and need volunteers, both people with Alzheimer's and healthy volunteers. Search ClinicalTrials.gov or ask the Alzheimer's Association Helpline about matching services.

Sources

  • Alzheimer's Association: 24/7 Helpline
  • Eldercare Locator
  • National Institute on Aging: Getting help with Alzheimer's caregiving
