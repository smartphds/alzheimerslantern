Questions to ask the doctor

Doctor visits go fast, and it's easy to forget what you wanted to ask. Print this list, check off the questions that matter to you, and bring it along.

By the Alzheimer's Lantern team · Last reviewed November 19, 2026 · 4-minute read

We are not medical professionals. This guide is general information, not medical advice. Please talk to a doctor about your own situation. In an emergency, call 911. Read our full disclaimer.

Before the visit

  • Write down the changes you've noticed, with examples and when they started.
  • Bring a list of all medicines, vitamins, and supplements, or bring the bottles.
  • Bring a family member or friend to take notes.
  • Ask for a longer appointment if you can.

About the diagnosis

  • What type of dementia is this? How sure are you?
  • What tests did you do, and what did they show? Can we have copies?
  • Could anything else be causing these symptoms, or making them worse?
  • What stage is this? What changes should we expect next?
  • Should we see a specialist, such as a neurologist, geriatrician, or memory clinic?

About treatment

  • What treatments could help? What are the benefits and side effects?
  • Are the newer medicines that slow Alzheimer's an option for us? See New Alzheimer's treatments in plain English.
  • Should any current medicines be stopped or changed?
  • Are there clinical trials we could join?
  • What can we do at home to help, such as exercise, sleep, or diet?

About daily life and safety

  • Is it still safe to drive? To cook? To manage medicines alone?
  • What should we do about wandering or getting lost?
  • What changes in behavior or health should we call you about?
  • Can we get a care plan? (Medicare covers a care-planning visit for people with memory problems.)

About support

  • Who can help us with services, such as a social worker or care manager?
  • Can you refer us to home health, therapy, or adult day programs?
  • Who do we call with questions between visits?

After the visit

  • Read through your notes while they're fresh.
  • Schedule the next follow-up before you leave.
  • Call the Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline, 1-800-272-3900, if you think of more questions.

Sources

  • National Institute on Aging: Next Steps After an Alzheimer's Diagnosis
  • Alzheimer's Association: Diagnosis of Alzheimer's Disease and Dementia
