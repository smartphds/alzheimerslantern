Paying for care: Medicare and Medicaid basics

Paying for Alzheimer's care is one of families' biggest worries. Here's what Medicare and Medicaid usually cover, the big gap many people don't expect, and where to get free help.

By the Alzheimer's Lantern team · Last reviewed November 19, 2026 · 7-minute read

We are not medical professionals. This guide is general information, not medical advice. Please talk to a doctor about your own situation. In an emergency, call 911. Read our full disclaimer.

The big surprise: long-term care

Many families assume Medicare will pay for help at home or a nursing home over the long term. It usually doesn't. Medicare does not pay for "custodial care," meaning ongoing help with daily activities like bathing, dressing, and eating, when that's the main care a person needs. Planning early for this gap is one of the most important things you can do.

Rules change and differ by state. Use this as a starting point, and check your own situation with the free help listed below.

What Medicare usually covers

  • Doctor visits, tests, and hospital stays
  • Memory evaluations and care planning. Medicare covers a visit to assess memory and thinking and make a care plan. Ask the doctor about a "cognitive assessment and care plan" visit.
  • Prescription drugs through Part D or a Medicare Advantage plan with drug coverage
  • Short-term skilled nursing after a hospital stay of at least 3 days, in a skilled nursing facility, for up to 100 days. You pay a daily amount after day 20.
  • Some home health care if the person is homebound and needs skilled care, such as nursing or therapy
  • Hospice care when a doctor believes the person likely has 6 months or less to live. This covers comfort care, medicines for symptoms, equipment, and support for the family.

Dementia care programs: Medicare has been testing a program called GUIDE that offers care coordination, caregiver training, and up to $2,500 a year for respite care for people with dementia, through participating providers. Ask the doctor or your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (see below) whether it's available near you.

What Medicaid can cover

Medicaid is for people with low income and limited savings. Unlike Medicare, it can pay for long-term care, including:

  • Nursing home care, for people who qualify
  • Home and community-based services in many states, such as in-home help and adult day programs, through "waiver" programs

Things to know:

  • Each state sets its own rules for income and savings limits.
  • Medicaid looks back at money and property given away in the 5 years before applying. Gifts in that time can delay coverage.
  • A spouse who stays at home is protected from losing everything. There are special rules to help them keep some income and savings.

Because the rules are complicated, many families meet with an elder law attorney before applying.

Other ways families pay

  • Personal savings and income
  • Long-term care insurance, if the person bought a policy. Check what it covers and how to file a claim.
  • Veterans' benefits. Veterans and surviving spouses may qualify for extra money for care through the VA's "Aid and Attendance" benefit.
  • Life insurance policies can sometimes be used for care.
  • Home equity, such as a reverse mortgage. Get independent advice first.

Where to get free, unbiased help

  • SHIP (State Health Insurance Assistance Program). Free, one-on-one Medicare counseling. Find yours at shiphelp.org.
  • Eldercare Locator: 1-800-677-1116. To find your local Area Agency on Aging, which knows local programs.
  • Alzheimer's Association 24/7 Helpline: 1-800-272-3900.
  • Your state Medicaid office, for how to apply.
